## Verified facts (fact-check 2026-10-13)

- Line 4 / on-screen '92×': Venus surface pressure is 'lagbhag 92 guna' Earth's. — CORRECT. The NSSDC lists the surface pressure as 92 bar. Against Earth's sea-level 1.013 bar that is ~91×, and NASA/ESA/Planetary Society figures range from 90× to 93×, so 'lagbhag 92 guna' is fine (it is also the original video's number).
- Line 5: 92× pressure ≈ about 900 m under the sea. — CORRECT. (92 - 1.01 bar) / (1025 kg/m3 × 9.81 m/s2) ≈ 905 m, and secondary sources quote NASA as '~3,000 ft (900-930 m)'. I could not open NASA's own page to confirm that wording (NASA hosts were unreachable from here), but the physics checks out. The popular 'a mile underwater' (~1.6 km ≈ 163 bar) overstates it, so leaving it out is right.
- Line 6 / on-screen '~465°C': surface temperature about 465 °C. — CORRECT. The NSSDC mean surface temperature is 737 K = 464 °C, and it is nearly the same day/night and pole/equator. The script's '~' and 'lagbhag' cover the 1 °C rounding. Saying 'degree' without 'Celsius' is fine for Indian viewers because the screen shows °C.
- Line 7: Mercury is closest to the Sun, but Venus is the hottest planet. — CORRECT. Mercury's dayside peaks at ~430 °C (800 °F) with nights near -180 °C, while Venus is ~465 °C everywhere. 'Hottest planet' means surface temperature, which is the standard usage (NASA, Guinness).
- Line 8: Venus has a very thick (bahut ghana) carbon dioxide atmosphere. — CORRECT. It is 96.5% CO2 and 3.5% N2. Surface air density is ~65 kg/m3, about 50× Earth's sea-level air, and the atmosphere's total mass is ~93× Earth's.
- Line 9: this atmosphere makes the heavy pressure and traps the heat. — CORRECT. The weight of the massive atmosphere gives the ~92 bar, and the CO2 runaway greenhouse traps the heat. This is also why Venus beats Mercury, which has almost no atmosphere.
- Line 10: 'Tum wahan zyada der zinda nahi reh paoge.' — CORRECT and not overclaimed. No time is given, and nothing about being crushed or melting in one second.
- Lines 0-1 and title: 'Venus par sirf ek second!' hook. — OK. It is only a framing device (taken from the original) and no line claims a specific survival time for a human. It never 'pays off', but that is a style point, not a factual one.
- Line 11 / on-screen: correct answer B, ~2 hours; 'VENERA 13, 1982 · 127 MIN · RECORD'. — CORRECT. Venera 13 landed on 1 March 1982 and transmitted from the surface for 127 min (2 h 7 min), against a design life of ~32 min. That beat Venera 12 (110 min); its twin Venera 14 lasted 57 min. No lander has reached Venus's surface since Vega 2 (1985), so it is still the record in 2026. '2 minute' and '2 din' are clearly wrong. Not saying why it stopped is the safe choice.
- Line 13: 'Ye poore planet jitna bada pressure cooker hai!' with chips PRESSURE ↑ · TEMP ↑. — OK as a metaphor (both high pressure and high temperature), kept from the original. Line 9 already gives the real cause (the CO2 greenhouse), so it does not suggest that pressure alone heats Venus.
- On-screen labels: VENUS = SHUKRA, MERCURY = BUDH. — CORRECT (शुक्र = Venus, बुध = Mercury).
- Spoken numbers: बानवे = 92, नौ सौ = 900, चार सौ पैंसठ = 465, दो घंटे = 2 hours. — CORRECT.
- word_count 118, est_seconds 40.8. — CORRECT. A recount of the Roman lines (numbers counted as spoken words, e.g. 'chaar sau painsath' = 3) gives 118; at 2.9 words/s that is ~40.7 s.
- Facts kept for the description / pinned comment: lead melts at ~327 °C (Venus is hotter); at 92 bar water boils at only ~305 °C. — CORRECT. Lead melts at 327.5 °C. From steam tables, water's saturation temperature at 9.2 MPa is ≈ 305 °C, so at 465 °C there is no liquid water on Venus, which makes the 'seeti kabhi nahi bajti' joke physically sound if it is used in the description.
- Footage caveat (not a script claim): the AI shots show a sharp Sun disc through the clouds and puffy cumulus clouds. — ARTISTIC, NOT REALISTIC. From Venus's surface the solid cloud deck hides the Sun's disc, and the light is a dim, diffuse orange. Nothing in the script claims otherwise, and the planned 'AI visual' tag covers it. Don't add any line like 'yahan se Sooraj aisa dikhta hai'.
- Verification method note. — NASA, Wikipedia and Guinness pages could not be fetched directly from this container (DNS blocked). The figures were checked through web-search results that quote those pages, plus physics calculations and standard reference values from my own knowledge.
